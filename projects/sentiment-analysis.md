Project 1: Text Classification and Sentiment Analysis

Project Overview

Project type: Practice annotation project
Task: Classify short text samples by sentiment
Dataset: Original examples created for this portfolio
Objective: Demonstrate consistent labeling, attention to detail, and clear annotation decisions.

Annotation Guidelines

Each text sample receives one sentiment label:

- Positive: Expresses satisfaction, happiness, praise, or approval.
- Negative: Expresses dissatisfaction, frustration, criticism, or disappointment.
- Neutral: Provides information without a clear positive or negative sentiment.

When a statement contains mixed opinions, consider its overall meaning rather than labeling it based on a single word.

Annotated Examples

ID| Text| Sentiment| Reasoning
001| The delivery arrived early, and everything was perfect.| Positive| The speaker expresses satisfaction with the delivery and order.
002| I waited two hours, but nobody attended to me.| Negative| The speaker expresses frustration about poor service.
003| My appointment is scheduled for Thursday at 10 a.m.| Neutral| The statement provides scheduling information without expressing an opinion.

006| The package contains three books and a notebook.| Neutral| The statement describes the contents of a package without an emotional judgment.
007| I am delighted with the support I received today.| Positive| The speaker explicitly expresses satisfaction.
008| The product looks good, although it costs more than I expected.| Mixed| The speaker expresses approval of the appearance but concern about the price.

Quality Assurance

Before submitting annotations, I would check that:

1. Every sample has a unique ID.
2. Labels follow the annotation guidelines.
3. The reasoning matches the meaning of the text.
4. Ambiguous or mixed-sentiment examples are flagged instead of forced into an unsuitable category.
5. The same labeling rules are applied consistently.

Key Learning

Sentiment annotation requires understanding the overall meaning of a statement. Some statements contain conflicting opinions, so a clear labeling policy is important for maintaining consistency.

Project Status

Completed as a self-directed practice exercise. This is an original demonstration dataset, not paid client work.
